JOSEPH A. TOLBERT
ATTORNEY AND COUNSELLOR AT LAW
GREENVILLE, S.C.

January 4th, 1934.

[Stamp: 251]

Mr. Roy Wilkins,
Asst. Secretary,
N.A.A.C. P.
69 Fifth Avenue,
New York City,

Dear Mr. Wilkins:

I have not heard from you since I wrote
you on December 21st. last as to the matter of assisting
the local Circuit Solicitor in the prosecution of the men
who lynched George Green in Greenville County on November
16th, 1933: and also the prosecution of the four chain
gang guards who are alleged to have beaten a colored convict
to death on the Greenville County chain gang last Autumn.

I understand from the Circuit Solicitor that
he expects to try the case of the convict guards at an early
date. The case against the other defendants will be tried later.

As indicated in my previous letter I would consider
a fee of two hundred and fifty dollars a reasonable fee in
each of the cases referred to above.Both cases I understand will
be hard fought. The defendants will have able counsel to represent
them in each case. I will thank you to advise me by return mail
fully in the premises. If your legal committee considers a fee
of two hundred and fifty dollars in each case too much, kindly
let me know what the fee is that is usually paid in such cases,
I do not want to charge an excessive fee in either case. I know
that I can render good service in the cases and I will do everything
in my power to see that justice is vindicate in each case, and that
the guilty parties are convicted of the two terrible crimes.

I shall thank you to advise me fully at your earliest
convenience. Let me know what your committee considers a satisfactory
fee in each case; and I feel certain that we can arrange the matter
of fees satisfactory to all parties concerned.

Kindly let me hear from you at once.

Very truly yours,
[signature]
Joseph A. Tolbert.

January 9, 1934

Dr. E. B. Burroughs
62 Hanover Street
Charleston, South Carolina

My dear Dr. Burroughs:

The National Office has agreed to assist in the prosecu-
tion of four white convict guards, who are charged with beating
to death a Negro chain gang prisoner near Greenville, South
Carolina.

These men are soon to go to trial, but the National
Office lacks sufficient funds to push the case as it should
be pushed.

This is a golden opportunity for the colored people of
South Carolina to assist in stamping out some of the well-known
brutality in the chain gang camps. Usually, it is very dif-
ficult to bring this brutality into court, but the white people
in Greenville have been so outraged over this killing that the
county authorities themselves have taken action against the
guards. Certainly, the colored people of the state, who have
so much at stake, should join enthusiastically in the prosecu-
tion of these men.

Another case is soon to come to trial in Greenville,
and that is the trial of the men who are charged with lynching
George Green on November 17, 1933. The National Office has
agreed to assist in the prosecution of these alleged lynchers.
The end of 1933 has found our treasury greatly reduced and
we must therefore appeal to our branches in South Carolina to
aid us in raising a fund to fight this case.

These two cases offer an opportunity for colored people,
in cooperation with the whites, to strike a blow at chain gang
brutality and at lynching. We cannot say in these two cases
that the white people have not done their part, because they
have arrested and indicted man for these crimes, and it is up
to us to see that the prosecution will be a vigorous one. We
hope you will raise money in some way at once and send your
contributions to the National Office without delay.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

January 9, 1934

Mr. E. C. L. Adams
907 Richland Street
Columbia, South Carolina

My dear Mr. Adams:

Mr. White has spoken of you and your interest in our work
so often, that I feel perfectly free to approach you, at his sug-
gestion, upon a matter which we have undertaken in Greenville,
South Carolina.

Perhaps you have read in the press that Greenville has in-
dicted a number of men on charges of participating in the lynch-
ing of one George Green on November 17, 1933. You may have read,
also, that four convict guards have been indicted in connection
with the beating to death of a Negro chain gang prisoner.

Mr. Joseph A. Tolbert of Greenville, former Republican
committeeman from South Carolina, has written us that the County
Solicitor has requested him to act as special prosecutor in these
two cases. Our Association finds itself, at the close of 1933,
with a greatly depleted general treasury and a legal defense
fund which is practically at the vanishing point. We have of-
fered Mr. Tolbert a very modest honorarium for his services in
these two cases, but we find that even that modest amount will
have to be raised, preferably in South Carolina. We have writ-
ten our branches in the state, asking them to raise and con-
tribute money toward this prosecution fund.

Mr. White desires me to ask you if you have any suggestions
on sources from which money may be raised. It seems to us that
these two cases present a golden opportunity for the colored
people of South Carolina and the responsible and decent white
people of the state to encourage the county officials at
Greenville in the excellent beginning they have made toward
stamping out chain gang brutality and lynching. The time is
short and the need is great. We will appreciate having your
suggestions at your earliest convenience.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

JOSEPH A. TOLBERT
ATTORNEY AND COUNSELLOR AT LAW
GREENVILLE, S. C.

January 11th, 1934.

[Stamp: 561]

Mr. Roy Wilkins,
Asst. Secretary
N. A. A. C. P.
69 Fifth Avenue,
New York City.

My dear Mr. Wilkins:

Your letter of January 9th advising me that
your legal committee had authorized you to employ me as
special prosecutor in the two cases two wit: the case of
four chain gang guards who are alleged to have beaten a
colored convict to death on the Greenville County chain gang,
and in the case of George Green who was lynched by a mob
in Greenville County on November 16th last was duly received.

Please be advised that I shall proceed to
render all possible assistance to the circuit solicitor in
the two cases on the basis outlined in your letter of January
9th,1934.

I had a conference with the circuit solicitor
this morning, and he informed me that he would make a special
effort to try the case of the State vs. the men who are
alleged to have killed George Green during the present
term of criminal court which is now in session here.
The circuit solicitor advised me that the Green case would
probably be tried next week.

I shall keep you advised of any developments
in the trial of both cases. I assure you that I appreciate
your cooperation in the matters; and I shall do all in my
power to help secure the vindication of justice in both cases.

Very sincerely yours,
[signature]
Joseph A. Tolbert,

AIR MAIL

January 9, 1934

Mr. Joseph A. Tolbert
Greenville, South Carolina

My dear Mr. Tolbert:

Our Legal Committee regrets that it can commit itself
for no more than $100 on each of the prosecutions; that is,
$100 for your work as special prosecutor in the case of the
four chain gang guards accused of beating a Negro prisoner
to death; and $100 for your work as special prosecutor in
the case of the men accused of lynching George Green, when
that trial is called.

As a matter of fact, we do not have this money in our
treasury at present and will have to campaign to raise it
from our membership.

The Legal Committee authorizes me to say that if you
will proceed on the basis of this amount, you may consider
this letter an agreement and we will forward some money on
the first $100 as soon as we can raise it.

We trust that you will keep us advised of the progress
of these cases, the date of the trials, etc.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

January 9, 1934

Dr. Will W. Alexander
Committee on Interracial Cooperation
703 Standard Building
Atlanta, Georgia

My dear Dr. Alexander:

We have decided to assist in the prosecution of four
white convict guards in Greenville, South Carolina, who are
charged with beating to death a colored chain gang prisoner.
The guards have been indicted by the county grand jury and the
county solicitor is desirous of pushing the case vigorously.
He has invited Joseph A. Tolbert, former Republican national
committeeman from South Carolina, to act as special prosecutor,
and we have agreed to supply Mr. Tolbert with a modest honora-
rium for his services.

We have decided, also, to assist in the prosecution of
the several men indicted in Greenville for alleged participa-
tion in the lynching of George Green on November 17, 1933. Mr.
Tolbert,also has been requested to act as special prosecutor
in this case and again we have agreed to a modest honorarium.

At the beginning of the new year, however, we find
ourselves with a greatly depleted treasury and with a legal
defense fund that is almost non-existent. We are having to
enlist the special assistance of our branches in South Caro-
lina and of certain individuals, and we are writing to ask
you if the Commission on Interracial Cooperation will not be
willing to assist in paying the modest commitment we have
made to Mr. Tolbert. We have offered him $100 for his work
in each case, a total of $200. We will be glad to have your
reaction to this proposal. We understand that the case
against the guards is to be called any day, but that the
lynching prosecution will be delayed for some time.

With good wishes for the New Year, I am

Sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

January 17, 1934

Mr. E. C. L. Adams
907 Richland Street
Columbia, South Carolina

My dear Mr. Adams:

Thank you for your frank letter of January 12 with
reference to the prosecutions at Greenville and the special
prosecutor engaged.

We were somewhat at a disadvantage in this matter
and had little or no choice in the selection of a special
prosecutor. We felt that the political activities of the
gentleman would militate against his effectiveness, but
we were faced with the question of whether we would turn
down an opportunity to have some part in the prosecution of
chain gang guards and lynchers when that opportunity had
been presented by the county itself. It is not likely
that we could have gone directly to the county authorities
and offered to provide a first-grade special prosecutor
with any chance of having our proposition accepted, so we
took the next best course.

I hope we may continue to count upon you for this
kind of advice as it is the only kind which is of any
value to us in proceeding in the South.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

The State of South Carolina
vs.
H.P. Dill, W.H. Forrester
C.B. Baker, and Julius Hightower,

My dear Mr. Wilkins:

The above entitled case was tried this week in
General Sessions Court for Greenville County, S. C. The trial
stated on Monday afternoon March 19th, and continued through
Wednesday March 21st.After receiving the charge of the Court
the jury deliberated all of Wednesday night and until Noon on
Thursday at which time the Court ordered a mistrial when the
jury failed to reach a verdict.

I feel that everything was done that could be done
to assist the Circuit Solicitor in the prosecution of the above
entitled case. We did our very best to have justice vindicated
and the guilty parties sufficiently punished.The case created
unusual interest in this section of the state. Public sentiment
seemed to be in favor of conviction of the defendants in the
above entitled case. The solicitor expects to try the case again
at another term of court.

I will appreciate the favor if you will kindly have
check forwarded to me in the sum of one hundred dollars ($100.00)
for services rendered in the above entitled case as per our contract.

Very sincerely yours,
[signature]
Joseph A. Tolbert,

March 29, 1934

Mr. James A. King
111 Read Street
Greenville, N. C.

My dear Mr. King:

We would like to have your advice on a situation in
Greenville. The trial of the four convict guards on the charge
of beating to death a colored chain gang prisoner has just been
completed, and,we understand,resulted in a mistrial.

This office, as you no doubt know, retained Mr. Joseph
A. Tolbert as special prosecutor in this case, and what we
would like to know from you is whether you believe, from the
sentiments you have heard expressed or have seen in the papers,
that it would have any good result or attain a conviction if
this Association retained a special prosecutor for the new
trial.

I should say that we are very poor and our Legal Com-
mittee is against spending any more money in this case unless
we are reasonably certain of securing a conviction. Of
course, you cannot say positively that a conviction can be
obtained; no one can. But you can inform us of the general
feeling in the town.

We would like to hear from you at your earliest con-
venience.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

April 26, 1934

Mr. Joseph A. Tolbert
Greenville, S. C.

My dear Mr. Tolbert:

I have your letter of April 21.

Our Legal Committee wishes me to express to you its
appreciation for the work which you did as special prosecutor
in the case of South Carolina vs. H. P. Dill et al. An ac-
count of this trial appeared in the New York Daily News for
Sunday, April 15, and it appeared from that account that the
case would have been won had it not been for the testimony of
several colored witnesses, among whom was a trusty, an under-
taker, and an embalmer. Even so, we note from your communica-
tions and the newspaper that sentiment was so overwhelming that
the jury could not arrive at a verdict.

We regret exceedingly that we have not been able to
forward to you the $100 which we agreed to pay for your ser-
vices in this case. As I explained to you at the time we
made the agreement, our funds for legal defense were (and
still are) very low. We wish to thank you for your indul-
gence thus far and for your suggestion that we send a portion
of this fee if we do not have the whole amount. We will make
every effort to send you a check for at least one half the
amount before May 10.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

May 10, 1934

Mr. Joseph A. Tolbert
Greenville, South Carolina

My dear Mr. Tolbert:

I have submitted your letter of May 1 to our Legal
Committee for its consideration of your offer to act as
special prosecutor in the retrial of South Carolina vs.
H. P. Dill, et al.

The Committee directs me to say to you that it
appreciates the work which you did in the first trial and
would like for you to write again submitting just what you
propose to do in the second trial. The Committee would like
some indication of the procedure you plan and some indica-
tion of the intensity of the activity in the retrial.

From the information we have,.it appears that sen-
timent is overwhelmingly in favor of a conviction. What
were the special obstacles encountered by the prosecution
in the first trial? What methods will be used to overcome
these obstacles in the second trial? How hard is the
Solicitor's Office working on the prosecution? What are
the chances for a conviction at the second trial?

The Committee seeks this information because, as
I have stated to you in our correspondence, our funds are
very low and while this is a most significant case and one
in which we would like to participate, we cannot allocate
additional funds to it without a fairly clear idea of what
is going to be done and the chances of success. The Com-
mittee invites you to write at your earliest convenience.

Very sincerely yours,

Assistant Secretary.

Dear Mr. Wilkins:

Your letter of recent date in reply to mine was
duly received, and contents carefully noted.

The above entitled case came on for trial in Court
of General Sessions at Greenville, S. C. on May 10th, 1934. The case
required three days to complete the trial.The case was hotly con-
tested throughout the trial. The same attorneys participated in the
second trial that appeared in the first trial of the case.

The Solicitor did everything in his powerto secure
a conviction of the defendants in the above entitled case. I rendered
all possible assistance.In my opinion the case would have been won for
the State had it not been for the judges charge to the jury.

The Statutes of South Carolina specifically provide
that if a county supervisor cannot control the convicts committed to his
custody then he should have such convicts either incarcerated in the
county jail or the state penetentiary. Judge Oxner the trial judge char-
ged the jury in the above entitled case that a supervisor had the legal
right to inflict corporal punishment, in controlling convicts committed
to his custody. In the light of the judges charge the jury returned a
verdict of acquittal.

Public sentiment and the fair minded people of Green-
ville County have always thought that the former convict guards beat
Roy Hudson to death; at least the brutal beating given by them caused
his death.The same sentiment prevails among the fair minded people to-
day. It was a terrible crime; and the men who committed the terrible
crime should have been punished for the terrible deed they committed.

If your legal committee is willing to do so I shall
appreciate whatever fee they are willing to pay me for assisting the
solicitor in the second trial of the above entitled case.

If convenient I shall thank you to forward me check
for the balance of $50.00 fee for trial of the case in March.

Very sincerely yours,
